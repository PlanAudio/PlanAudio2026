Unified Synthesis of Compositional Speech and Sound from Free-Form Text Prompts

Code

Abstract

Audio generation has seen significant progress, yet synthesizing unified audio where speech and environmental sounds are naturally composited remains a challenge. Current methods either rely on disjoint pipelines that fail to capture fine-grained interactions or require structured inputs and external text rewriting, limiting the flexibility of free-form text prompts. In this paper, we introduce a new task: Free-Form-Text-Prompt-to-Unified-Audio generation, which aims to directly synthesize unified audio containing speech, sound, and their composites from unconstrained natural language. To address this task, we propose PlanAudio, an encoder-free, autoregressive LLM-based framework. PlanAudio leverages the intrinsic language understanding of LLMs and introduces a Semantic latent chain-of-thought (Latent CoT) mechanism. This mechanism enables the model to perform implicit semantic planning in a continuous latent space before audio generation, effectively bridging high-level semantic understanding and low-level acoustic synthesis. Furthermore, we create PlanAudio-Bench, a specialized benchmark for evaluating composite audio scenarios. We conduct evaluation across the scenarios of speech, sound, and their compositions. Results demonstrate that PlanAudio outperforms existing pipeline and unified baselines. Our analysis further reveals the superiority of Semantic Latent CoT over other CoT mechanisms and highlights the importance of continuous multi-scenario training curricula.
